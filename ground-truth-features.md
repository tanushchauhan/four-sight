In the event of a natural disaster like an earthquake, a rescuer can strap on our custom 3D printed visor, put on a glove controller, and tap in to see a direct feed from the robot itself.

To control the robot directly, the rescuer can tilt his head to moving the camera and then use the joystick to pivot forward, backward, up, down. The interface uses SLAM to identify features of survivors as well as infared, light sensors, and other sensors for dangerous conditions and supporting the robot on its task.

When the robot finds a survivor. The rescuer can comfort and notify the survivor through a microphone attached on the robot. Looking through the visor’s front view, the rescuer can see where they need to go from the camera in front of their visor.

These robots are designed to be autonomous, and their true purpose is to be sent into swarms around an impacted location. A nemotron agent coordinates commands sent through voice by the rescuer where they can attend to other duties while the visor is on. The agent will prioritize survivors in critical conditions, has a queue based system for survivors.
